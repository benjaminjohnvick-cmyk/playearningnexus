# Founding Advertiser Offer — Legal & Compliance Review Packet

**Prepared for review by qualified securities / FTC / consumer-finance counsel. This is NOT legal advice.**
It is a plain-language description of a proposed offer and the issues its operator has already tried to
design around, so counsel can assess it efficiently. Nothing here should launch, and no money should be
collected, until counsel has reviewed the offer, the mechanics, and every piece of marketing copy.

---

## 1. What the offer is

A limited **"Founding Advertiser"** program on a closed-loop, 18+ play-to-earn / survey-rewards platform
(GamerGain / PlayEarning Nexus). Key terms as configured:

- **Seats:** capped at 100,000 (a scarcity/marketing limit).
- **Price:** $8,000, paid **once, upfront**, and **non-refundable** ("presale" funds model).
- **Term:** a 4-year founding package.
- **What the buyer is buying:** advertising (a fixed, stated allotment of ~200,000 "between-survey" ad
  impressions per year, delivered across the platform's interstitial and social surfaces) **plus** membership
  in the closed-loop rewards ecosystem. Advertising begins delivering once the platform launches.
- **Funding purpose:** the operator intends to use the non-refundable proceeds to build and launch the
  platform and acquire users — i.e., a crowdfunding-style pre-sale.
- **Launch milestone (single gate):** the platform "launches" only once **100,000 founding members** exist.
  In the first-year offer, **founders are also the users** (each founder signs up, uses the site, and does
  surveys for the year), so there is one count of 100,000 people — not 100,000 founders plus a separate
  100,000 users.
- **Members as earners:** every founding member is enrolled as a user who can earn rewards by completing
  surveys. Rewards are **Site Cash** — closed-loop, non-cashable **store credit** spendable only on the
  platform. There is **no cash-out** of Site Cash to bank/card/P2P.

## 2. The mechanics that carry the most legal weight

1. **Non-refundable presale.** The $8,000 is not held in escrow and is not refundable; if the platform never
   launches, the buyer does not get their money back. (An escrow/refundable "escrow" model and a "hybrid"
   partial-deposit model are also built and switchable, but the operator has selected non-refundable presale.)
   The offer page shows a prominent red **non-refundable risk warning** and requires an explicit acceptance
   checkbox before a seat can be reserved; acceptance is recorded in an append-only consent ledger.

2. **"Full-keep" survey benefit, capped.** As members, founding advertisers keep **100% of what they earn**
   from surveys (vs. a smaller standard share), **up to a cumulative cap** and over a **window** (defaults:
   cap = the amount they paid = $8,000; window = 4 years), after which they revert to the standard rate.
   - The cap is metered per member; the benefit is wired into all survey-credit paths.
   - It applies during the offer year, after launch, **and in the failure case**: if the platform does not hit
     the milestone, a founding member can still earn store credit at the founding rate by completing
     **third-party** surveys, up to the cap, over 4 years, **as long as the site operates**.

3. **Earnings are variable, not guaranteed, and paid only as store credit.** The operator has deliberately
   avoided: any guaranteed financial return (2x/4x etc.); any "zero-risk / guaranteed profit" language; any
   auto-charge of a member's card for an earnings shortfall; and any separate cash grant. Member survey
   earnings are labeled variable and not guaranteed everywhere they appear.

4. **Participation + feedback.** Members are told they participate for the year and that the operator will
   solicit feedback via surveys to refine the product. Framed as participation, with no penalty for a
   shortfall.

## 3. Issues for counsel — and how the design already tries to address each

The operator understands these are the operator's questions to resolve with counsel, not settled positions.

### (a) Securities / investment contract (Howey)
- **Risk:** a person pays $8,000 with a structure that lets them "recoup" that amount over 4 years. A cap set
  *equal to the amount paid* is a return-of-capital signal, and "expectation of profit from the efforts of
  others" is the core of an investment contract.
- **Mitigations built in:** the buyer is purchasing **advertising + membership**, not a financial instrument;
  **no financial return is promised or guaranteed**; the "recoup" value is **store credit, not cash**, and is
  earned through the **member's own survey labor** (weakening the "efforts of others" prong); and all copy is
  written as a **variable, capped benefit**, never as recoupment.
- **For counsel:** Does the cap-equals-price design, even with these mitigations, still risk being treated as
  an investment contract or a "note"? Would decoupling the cap from the exact price reduce risk? Are state
  blue-sky laws implicated?

### (b) FTC earnings claims / Business Opportunity Rule
- **Risk:** statements like "8 minutes a day to earn $8," "keep $8/day," or "earn your money back over 4 years"
  are earnings claims. Survey inventory cannot guarantee those amounts, so an unqualified claim is likely
  unsubstantiated/deceptive; the overall "pay to participate and earn" shape may implicate the Business
  Opportunity Rule.
- **Mitigations built in:** every earnings figure is qualified as **variable, availability-dependent, and not
  guaranteed** ("~8 minutes *when surveys are available*; not a promise you'll earn $8 in 8 minutes"); no
  income guarantee; no promise of recoupment.
- **For counsel:** What substantiation is required to state any earnings figure at all? Do we need typical/
  average-results disclosures? Does the Business Opportunity Rule (or state equivalents) apply, and if so what
  disclosure document is required?

### (c) Crowdfunding / pre-sale delivery obligations (FTC)
- **Risk:** taking non-refundable money to "build then deliver" is a crowdfunding-style pre-sale; the FTC has
  acted against operators who took funds without a good-faith effort to deliver.
- **Mitigations built in:** funds are earmarked for the stated purpose (build/launch); the non-refundable /
  may-not-launch risk is disclosed prominently and accepted; records are kept.
- **For counsel:** What obligations attach to the non-refundable pre-sale? Is any portion legally required to
  be refundable or escrowed? What record-keeping and delivery-effort standard should we hold ourselves to?

### (d) Money transmission / stored value / holding customer funds
- **Risk:** collecting up to $8,000 × 100,000 = ~$800M, and issuing closed-loop store credit, can raise
  money-transmitter / stored-value questions.
- **Mitigations built in:** Site Cash is **closed-loop and non-cashable** (no redemption for cash, no P2P
  transfer), which is the classic posture for staying outside money transmission; funds are the operator's
  revenue in the presale model (not held for the customer).
- **For counsel:** Do any state stored-value/money-transmitter statutes apply to the store credit or to
  holding the pre-sale proceeds? Does the escrow/hybrid model (if we switch to it) change the analysis?

### (e) Consumer protection / non-refundable & auto-renewal
- **Mitigations built in:** prominent non-refundable disclosure + explicit consent; **no negative-option
  billing and no auto-charge** of any kind in the founding offer.
- **For counsel:** Are the non-refundable terms enforceable in our target states? Any required cooling-off /
  cancellation rights?

### (f) Advertising deliverability & the "Ponzi" line
- The impression allotment is a fixed, stated deliverable. The operator has been explicit that **no promised
  payout to earlier buyers may ever be funded out of later buyers' money** — member earnings come from real
  survey demand (third parties), not from new founding intake. Please confirm the structure cannot be
  characterized as paying returns from new deposits.

### (g) Data & privacy
- The offer involves surveys and feedback collection; a separate privacy review covers consent, data
  minimization, and the platform's aggregate-only insights posture.

## 4. What is intentionally NOT in the offer (to stay clear of the above)

- No guaranteed financial return (no 2x/4x, no "double your money").
- No "zero risk / guaranteed profit" claim.
- No 2%-of-profit fee or any profit-sharing that implies an investment.
- No auto-charging a card for an earnings shortfall.
- No separate cash/points grant; the only value kept is variable survey earnings as store credit.
- No promise that any member will reach the cap or "earn their money back."

## 5. Operator controls (feature flags) counsel should know exist

These settings gate the risky pieces and let the operator implement counsel's guidance without new code:

- Funds model: `FOUNDING_FUNDS_MODEL` = presale | escrow | hybrid (currently **presale**).
- Full-keep cap: `FOUNDING_FULLKEEP_CAP_TO_PRICE` (cap = amount paid), `FOUNDING_FULLKEEP_CAP_USD`,
  `FOUNDING_FULLKEEP_YEARS` (4).
- No shortfall charge: `FOUNDING_MEMBER_SHORTFALL_CHARGE` = **off** (must stay off).
- Milestone: `FOUNDING_LAUNCH_MILESTONE_FOUNDERS` (100,000); `FOUNDING_LAUNCH_MILESTONE_PREMIUM_USERS` = 0
  (founders are the users).
- Survey earn share: `FOUNDING_SURVEY_EARN_SHARE_PCT` (1.0 = keep 100%, up to the daily cap).

## 6. The specific asks for counsel

1. Is this offer, as structured, an unregistered security or a regulated business opportunity? If so, what
   changes (e.g., decouple the cap from the price; remove recoup framing entirely; register/qualify) make it
   compliant?
2. What earnings-claim substantiation and disclosures are mandatory for any figure we show?
3. What are our obligations and required disclosures for a non-refundable crowdfunding-style pre-sale, and
   should any portion be escrowed/refundable?
4. Do money-transmitter / stored-value rules apply to the store credit or the held funds in any target state?
5. Please review and mark up the exact member-facing copy (the offer page and disclosures) before launch.
